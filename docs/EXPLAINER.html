Follow one invoice through the system.

CLI and browser call the same service. Agents extract and propose decisions. Code checks the facts and controls whether a payment can happen.

01 / READCapture the source

Read native PDF text once per page. Use local OCR for scans. Record unreadable content.

02 / EXTRACTRead, critique, retry

Build a typed invoice. Retry a bounded number of times with specific complaints.

03 / VERIFYCheck source and business

Compare fields, preserve repeated rows, recompute money, aggregate stock, check duplicates.

04 / DECIDEMake the outcome explicit

Approve and reserve, reject a business error, or open a persistent review alert.

PAID

Verified extraction, accepted business checks, approval, and a successful simulated payment.

REVIEW_REQUIRED

Business-valid extraction with insufficient evidence. No payment; a reviewer can verify and reprocess.

REJECTED / FAILED

A business rule blocks the invoice, or processing failed. Extraction uncertainty also opens a review alert.

One SQLite database stores source snapshots, extracted values, findings, confidence, alerts, decisions, and payment records. A correction gets its own linked run.

Scores are deterministic evidence ratings, not probabilities. The weakest checked field sets the invoice score. The offline extractor and verifier share parsing code, so agreement alone is not independent proof of accuracy.

Why the missing currency matters.

The sample CSV invoice_1006.csv contains a total of 2,750 but no currency code or symbol. The previous system assumed USD and paid it.

It now scores the currency at 85, below the default threshold of 90. A human must establish the missing fact before payment. This is a policy change, not a claim that the parser became more accurate.

Scanned pages take a conservative path too: OCR reads them, and a reviewer verifies the result until a broader scan benchmark supports automation.

A useful distinction

QuestionCheck
Did we read the invoice correctly?Source values, row coverage, page methods, conflicts, missing fields.
Is the invoice acceptable?Arithmetic, catalog, aggregated stock, duplicate identity, approval policy.
Who verified an uncertainty?Reviewer name, note, before/after fields, and a linked correction run.

A perfectly extracted invoice can still be rejected. A plausible invoice can still need review.

Human review closes the loop.

What the operator does

  1. Open the alert and original document.
  2. Verify every payment field and line. Obtain missing facts from a trusted business source.
  3. Correct the fields, record a name and reason, and explicitly confirm verification.
  4. Reprocess; inspect the new decision and its link to the original.

What the system preserves

The original machine extraction, score, and outcome stay intact. An atomic claim prevents two reviewers from resolving the same alert. The new run repeats the business checks.

The machine score is not inflated after review. The report separately records human verification. Closing an alert without payment is an explicit alternative.

Reviewer names are recorded, not authenticated in this local prototype.

The questions worth being ready for.

Can a model override a failed check?

No. Blocking business findings reject the invoice before approval. Uncertain source evidence holds it before payment. High-value invoices with unresolved warnings are also gated in code.

How do you prevent a concurrent double payment?

A SQLite transaction reserves invoice identity before calling the mock payment. A second run cannot acquire that reservation. Real bank integration still needs provider-side idempotency, reconciliation, and crash recovery.

Why not trust the model's own confidence?

A model can confidently invent values. The score here comes from deterministic source corroboration and structural checks. It has explicit limitations and is not presented as a calibrated probability.

Is this production-ready?

It is an inspectable prototype with tested safeguards. Production needs authentication and roles, a durable job worker, external alert delivery, restart/reconciliation tooling, retention controls, and a representative benchmark. In-app alerts are implemented; email and Slack delivery are not.
